# Wrap-Up: Examining Distributions

You have now completed the module on examining distributions, which is the first of two modules within the EDA unit. You've seen how, by using graphs and numbers, we can bring to light all the information that is stored in the data about the variable of interest. Another important point is that graphs and numbers are powerful only when accompanied by their interpretation in the context of the problem.

To put your knowledge into practice, your instructor may assign a data-analysis activity in which you use statistical software or a calculator to explore a real dataset: creating appropriate graphs, computing numerical summaries, and—most importantly—interpreting what they tell you in context.

## Reflect

Before moving on, take a moment to reflect on what you've learned:

- Given a new dataset, could you decide whether a pie chart, bar chart, histogram, stemplot, or boxplot is the appropriate display?
- Could you describe a distribution's shape, center, and spread from its histogram, and identify possible outliers?
- Do you know when to summarize a distribution with the mean and standard deviation, and when to use the five-number summary instead?

If you're unsure about any of these, revisit the relevant pages in this section before continuing to the next module, where we examine *relationships* between two variables.
