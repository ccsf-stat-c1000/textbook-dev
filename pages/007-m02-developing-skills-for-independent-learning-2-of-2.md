# Developing Skills for Independent Learning (2 of 2)

## Learn By Doing

Now take the opportunity to practice the concepts you've been learning. Read each of the scenarios below and identify which metacognitive skill the student is struggling with. If you need help, remember that you can ask for a hint.

:::{quiz} The night before a statistics project is due, Sam finally reads the instructions and discovers it requires collecting data from 30 people—something he cannot possibly do in one evening. Which metacognitive skill did Sam neglect?
:hint: Sam's problem started before he ever began working: he never figured out what the project involved.
:feedback-0: Correct! Sam never got a handle on what the task required or the time it would take—he failed to assess the task.
:feedback-1: There's no evidence Sam misjudged his own abilities; he simply never looked at what the task required.
:feedback-2: Sam never made a plan to monitor. His problem came earlier in the cycle.
:feedback-3: Reflection happens after a task. Sam's problem happened before he even started.
* *Assess the task
* Evaluate his strengths and weaknesses
* Apply strategies and monitor his performance
* Reflect and adjust
:::

:::{quiz} Leah did well in her high school math classes, so she decides to skip the algebra review at the start of the course. A week later she realizes she cannot follow the formulas in the reading. Which metacognitive skill did Leah struggle with?
:hint: Leah made a judgment about her own knowledge—and it turned out to be too optimistic.
:feedback-0: Leah understood what the review covered; her mistake was about herself, not the task.
:feedback-1: Correct! Leah misjudged her own skills and knowledge in relation to the task—she did not accurately evaluate her strengths and weaknesses.
:feedback-2: Leah's plan (skip the review) failed because it was built on a misjudgment of her own knowledge.
:feedback-3: Noticing the problem a week in is actually monitoring at work—the earlier failure was in evaluating her own preparation.
* Assess the task
* *Evaluate her strengths and weaknesses
* Plan an approach
* Reflect and adjust
:::

## Did I Get This?

:::{quiz} Which of the following best describes metacognition?
:hint: Think about the definition: "thinking about thinking."
:feedback-0: Memorization is a study technique, not awareness and regulation of your own learning.
:feedback-1: Correct! Metacognition is your awareness of yourself as a learner and your ability to regulate your own learning.
:feedback-2: Working quickly is not the point—experts actually spend more time planning, not less.
* Memorizing material so well that you can repeat it without thinking
* *Being aware of yourself as a learner and regulating your own learning
* Completing tasks as quickly as possible to leave time for review
:::

:::{quiz} While studying for an exam, you pause and compare how much you've covered with where your study plan says you should be by now. Which metacognitive skill are you using?
:hint: You are in the middle of the work, checking progress against a goal.
:feedback-0: Assessing the task happens before you start working.
:feedback-1: Planning happens before you start; you are already underway.
:feedback-2: Correct! Comparing where you are to where you want to be, while working, is monitoring your performance.
:feedback-3: Reflecting happens after the task is finished.
* Assess the task
* Plan an approach
* *Apply strategies and monitor your performance
* Reflect and adjust
:::

Strong metacognitive skills are essential for independent learning, so use the experience of monitoring your own learning in this course as an opportunity to hone these skills for other classes and tasks.
