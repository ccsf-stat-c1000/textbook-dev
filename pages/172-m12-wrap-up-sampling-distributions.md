# Wrap-Up (Sampling Distributions)

As mentioned in the introduction, this last module in probability is the bridge between the probability section and inference. It focuses on the relationship between sample values ({term}`statistics <statistic>`) and population values ({term}`parameters <parameter>`). Statistics vary from sample to sample due to *sampling variability*, and therefore can be regarded as {term}`random variables <random variable>` whose distribution we call the {term}`sampling distribution`. In this module we focused on two statistics, the {term}`sample proportion`, $\hat{p}$, and the {term}`sample mean`, $\bar{X}$. Our goal was to explore the sampling distribution of these two statistics relative to their respective population parameters (p and $\mu$), and we found in *both* cases that under certain conditions the *sampling distribution is approximately normal*. This result is known as the *Central Limit Theorem.* As we'll see in the next section, the Central Limit Theorem is the foundation for statistical inference.
